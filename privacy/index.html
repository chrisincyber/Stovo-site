Privacy Policy

Effective 3 November 2026 · Applies to Stovo on iPhone, iPad and Apple Watch · Deutsch

In short: Stovo works without an account. Without one, your data stays on your device. If you sign in, Stovo syncs your data between your devices and lets you share a household or a list; it is then stored on Stovo’s server, a Supabase project in Zurich, Switzerland. Your loyalty cards, recipe folders, star ratings and private notes are visible only to you, even inside a shared household. Stovo shows no ads, does not track you and sends no analytics unless you turn on optional usage statistics. A few small things leave your device, and each is explained below: store logos are loaded from Logo.dev; if you ask for a store to be added, the store name you typed is sent to us; when you scan a product, a barcode is looked up, and product facts you add from a package go to Stovo’s shared catalogue; if you turn on usage statistics (off until you do), simple daily counts go to PostHog; if you use voice entry on a language your iPhone cannot recognize on the device, your voice goes to Apple, and only after you agree. Stovo AI uses ChatGPT (OpenAI) for recipes, ideas and open conversation: it sees your message and the names on your lists, Pantry and recipes, never amounts or notes. The assistant says so once when you first open it, and you can turn it off in Settings.

Who is responsible

Stovo is developed and published by its owner. For any privacy question, write to info@stovo.ch.

What Stovo stores

Stovo stores what you enter into it so the app can work:

  • your shopping lists and their items, with amounts, units and notes;
  • your pantry: products, amounts, the batches they came in, where they are kept (Fridge, Pantry or Other), and optional details you add, such as expiration dates and purchase prices;
  • your purchase history — what was checked off and when — so a purchase can be undone;
  • your recipes, your meal plan, who is eating, and your star ratings, private notes, cooking dates and recipe folders;
  • your household’s name, your own categories, and your choices for products, such as the category or storage location you picked;
  • your loyalty and membership cards, if you add any (see Loyalty cards).

All of this is stored on your device, and Stovo works without an account. If you sign in, it is also stored on Stovo’s server (see Stovo’s server). Stovo does not ask for your location or contacts, and asks for your email address only if you sign in. It uses the camera only when you scan a card or a product, and photos only when you choose one yourself.

Your account (optional)

Signing in is optional. Without an account, nothing on this page about Stovo’s server applies to you.

  • Sign in with Apple. Apple tells Stovo a stable account identifier and, depending on your choice, your email address or Apple’s private relay address. Stovo asks Apple for your email address only, not your name.
  • Email code. You enter an email address, and Stovo’s server sends a six-digit code to it.
  • Display name. You choose a name of 2 to 24 characters that the other members of your household see. Email addresses are never shown to other members.
  • Stovo’s sign-in service is Supabase Auth. It stores your account identifier, your email address (or relay address) and the sessions needed to keep you signed in.

Stovo’s server (only after you sign in)

When you sign in, Stovo syncs your data with its server, a Supabase project in Zurich, Switzerland, which processes it on Stovo’s behalf. What is stored there, and who can see it:

  • Members of your household: shopping lists and items (with amounts, units and notes), products, the Pantry (amounts, batches, storage place, best-before dates), purchase history, categories and preferences; recipes shared with the household (text, ingredients, steps, tags and one photo), the meal plan, who is eating, routines, away days and leftovers; the household’s name, its members, invitation tokens (stored hashed, valid for one week) and the household’s Premium expiry date.
  • The people you gave a link to: a single list you shared, and that list only.
  • Only you: your loyalty cards (name, code, note and optional pictures); your recipe folders and favourites, star ratings, private notes, cooking dates and “Only me” recipes; your choice of Pantry for shared lists, and your unit and currency settings.

“Only you” is enforced by the server’s access rules (row-level security): a row can be read only by the account it belongs to. One exception: when a meal in the shared plan is marked as cooked, that mark belongs to the household plan, and your household sees it.

Data is sent over encrypted connections (HTTPS). It is not end-to-end encrypted: Stovo does not add its own encryption on top, and the hosting provider stores it on Stovo’s behalf. Like any hosting platform, it keeps routine technical logs, such as time and IP address.

Sharing a household

The owner of a household creates an invitation link (stovo.ch/join/…, valid for one week). Anyone who opens it and signs in joins as a member and can see and edit the household’s lists, pantry, purchase history, shared recipes and meal plan. Members see the display names people chose, never email addresses. A member can leave at any time in Stovo’s Settings; the owner can remove members or close the household for everybody.

Sharing a single list

You can also share just one shopping list by link. The people you invite see and edit only that list and the items on it. They do not see your pantry, purchase history, categories or other lists. If you are invited to a list, what you buy from it goes to the Pantry you choose; that choice is private to you and the list’s owner and the other people on the list cannot see it.

Apple Watch

The Stovo app on Apple Watch syncs shopping and the Pantry directly with Stovo’s server over HTTPS, so it works when your iPhone is away. Your iPhone hands the watch a short-lived access token. The watch syncs no cards, recipe folders, ratings or notes, and it collects no usage statistics. A watch face or Smart Stack widget shows a count of items left and a list name, or a shortcut to a card by its name. It never shows item details, codes or card numbers.

Loyalty cards come to the watch from your iPhone, directly, over the connection between the two (Bluetooth or Wi-Fi), as finished pictures of the codes. If Keep Cards on This Device is on, cards are sent to the watch only if you switch on Cards on Apple Watch in Settings. Turning that off, or deleting a card, removes it from the watch the next time the two can connect.

Loyalty cards

Stovo can keep your loyalty and membership cards. A card consists of a name, the code type and the code’s content (the number), an optional note, whether it is a favourite, and optionally a picture you choose. Stovo is not a payment wallet and does not store payment cards.

  • Camera and photos. To scan a card, Stovo asks iOS for camera access. To import a screenshot, you choose the picture with the system photo picker, so Stovo sees only the picture you pick. The code is read on your device. The camera image and the screenshot are not kept or sent anywhere; Stovo keeps only the code it reads and, for a code it cannot redraw faithfully, a cut-out picture of that barcode.
  • Where cards are stored. On your device. If you are signed in, your cards are also kept on Stovo’s server, in your own account only. They are never part of a shared household or list: people you share with never see your cards. They are not end-to-end encrypted.
  • Keep Cards on This Device. In Settings you can keep cards on the device only. Then nothing about your cards goes to Stovo’s server.
  • Which cards you open and when is remembered on the device only, to show recent cards first.

Card logos (Logo.dev)

To show a store’s logo on a card, Stovo asks Logo.dev (logo.dev) for the logo by the store’s website address, for example “migros.ch”. That request carries the store’s website address, Stovo’s public access key and, as with any web request, your device’s IP address and basic technical details such as the app’s user agent. It never contains your card number, the barcode, the name you gave the card, or any account information. Logo.dev returns the image, and your device keeps it for up to a day. If logos cannot be loaded, Stovo shows the store’s initials instead and everything else works the same. Logo.dev handles requests under its own privacy policy. Logos provided by Logo.dev.

Asking for a store to be added

If you search for a store in Add Card, don’t find it and tap the request button, Stovo sends us the store name as you typed it, your device’s country and language setting, and the app version, over an encrypted connection. The screen shows you what is sent before you send it. It contains nothing that identifies you or your device: no account, no identifier and no card data, and we do not link it to you. We use it only to decide which stores to add. The request is received by a small service run for Stovo, hosted on Vercel, and forwarded to us by email through Resend. If you are offline, the request waits on your device and is sent later.

Recipes

  • Import from a link. Stovo makes one request for the page you shared, from your device, directly to that website (no cookies, no account). The website sees your IP address and the address requested, as if you had opened the link in a browser. Nothing goes to Stovo or to an AI service. Links from Instagram are not requested; paste the caption text instead.
  • Import from pictures or a PDF. Text in pictures and in picture-only PDF pages is read on your device with Apple’s text recognition. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Photos. A recipe photo you choose is stored with the recipe, and on Stovo’s server if you are signed in.
  • Quick Add from text, Meal Planner Quick Add and Voice Quick Add read what you type or say with rules on your device and show you a preview. They send nothing to any server (for voice, see Voice entry).
  • Nutrition estimates for a recipe are calculated on your device from the ingredients, using Swiss Food Composition Database figures that are part of the app.

Calendar (optional)

If you allow it, Stovo writes your planned meals as events to a calendar on your phone and keeps them up to date. It only changes events it created. The events are stored by your calendar account (for example iCloud or Google) under that provider’s terms. Stovo does not send them to its own server for this purpose.

Product barcodes and the shared catalogue

In Shopping and the Pantry, Add Item has a Scan product button that looks a packaged product up by its barcode. A Pantry item that has a barcode also has a Product Information row, which shows what is known about the product. Looking a product up is your choice: nothing is looked up until you scan or tap Product Information, and scanning alone never submits anything to the catalogue. Your lists, pantry, purchases, household and scan history are never sent to the catalogue or to Open Food Facts; they stay on your devices and, if you sign in, in your account as described above. Stovo keeps no scan history: no list of what you scanned or when. It only remembers the product facts it found, on your device (see the next point).

  • Looking up a product. A product is looked up when you scan its barcode, and when you open Product Information for a Pantry item that has a barcode (only when you tap it). If Stovo has already saved the product’s details on your device, it uses those and may not need to look anything up; if you are offline it shows what is saved. Otherwise Stovo sends the barcode number and the package market (a country code: your device’s region unless you choose another country) to Stovo’s product catalogue. Stovo also asks Open Food Facts, a community database whose data is available under the Open Database License, when the catalogue has nothing for your package market, and when the catalogue has the product but without details such as scores or nutrition (unless you have switched off both Show Product Scores and Show Nutrition Facts in Settings → Products & Nutrition). That request sends only the barcode number and the language Stovo is shown in, straight from your phone to Open Food Facts (not through Stovo), so Open Food Facts sees your IP address like any website. Your lists, pantry and household are never sent. As with any web request, both also see your device’s IP address. A lookup needs no sign-in and carries no identifier. Open Food Facts runs its own servers and keeps its own logs under its own policy. The answer is saved on your device, in a cache that is not backed up, so the same barcode is not asked again straight away. When Open Food Facts has them, the answer can include the product’s Nutri-Score and how it was worked out, its NOVA food processing group, its nutrient levels (low, moderate or high for fat, saturated fat, sugars and salt), diet information estimated from its ingredient list (for example vegetarian or vegan), labels printed on its package, and its additives. Stovo shows these as Open Food Facts supplies them, does not calculate or rate them, and asks for the product’s name and ingredients in the language Stovo is shown in, so that language is part of the request. They are saved only in that same cache on your device and are not sent anywhere. You can hide them with the Show Product Scores setting in Settings → Products & Nutrition.
  • Where Stovo’s catalogue runs. The catalogue is a database on Supabase, hosted in Switzerland (Zurich), which processes it on Stovo’s behalf. It holds the product facts people added, and nothing about households. Like any hosting platform, it keeps routine technical request logs, such as time and IP address.
  • Adding product facts from a package. If a scanned product is not in the catalogue, or its details are wrong, you can type in what the package says. When you tap Add on that form, the facts are shared; the form says so. Only that button submits anything: scanning a product does not. Stovo then stores in its shared catalogue, with the barcode: the product’s name (only when you typed or changed it, or confirmed that it matches the package), brand and size, whether it is food or not, the ingredients, the allergens you ticked, any nutrition values, the package market (a country code) and your device’s language code. Other Stovo users see them straight away when they look that barcode up, labelled as community information: “Community submitted” for the name, brand and size, and “Unverified community information” for the rest, until others confirm them. Because the catalogue answers a lookup for any exact barcode, the facts can technically be seen by anyone who asks for that barcode, not only by people who use the app. A different value for a fact the catalogue already has waits for review and is not shown. Stovo checks submissions for spam and impossible values and may hold back, change or remove facts, for example when two submissions disagree or a fact is reported as wrong. You cannot edit or delete a submission yourself in the app. Facts you add are also kept for you on your device, even if the catalogue refuses them or the upload fails; a failed upload is not sent again automatically.
  • “I checked this package”. You can confirm facts you checked on the package without typing them again. Stovo then sends which facts you ticked (for example “allergens” or “fat”), the package market, and the product’s barcode, not the facts themselves. Report a problem sends the product’s barcode and a reason such as “wrong allergens”, with no free text.
  • What “Community confirmed” means. A fact is marked “Community confirmed” when at least two other app identities, different from the one that added it, entered or confirmed the same value for the same product and package market. This does not prove that they are different people, and it is not an official check by Stovo or anyone else.
  • Not shared. Amounts you have at home, expiration dates, lists, your household, notes, prices, purchases, scan history, photos, your name, your email address and your Apple Account.

The random sign-in used for sharing

Looking up products needs no sign-in. The first time you add product facts, confirm facts or report a problem, Stovo creates a random sign-in with its catalogue server. It has no name, email address or password and is not linked to your Apple Account or to your Stovo account. It is kept in the iOS Keychain on your device only (it is not synced or restored on another device). There is no setting to turn it off: it is created only when you add, confirm or report, and the server deletes it after 180 days without use (below). It exists for two purposes: to limit abuse (for example, too many submissions), and to count confirmations from separate app identities.

How long the server keeps things:

  • With each contribution, report or confirmation the server stores only a one-way hash of the sign-in, never the sign-in itself. Counters used for limiting abuse are deleted after 7 days. Hashes linked to contributions, reports and confirmations are removed after 30 days, or up to 90 days after the last confirmation while a fact is still waiting for confirmation or a different suggestion is waiting for review. After that, Stovo cannot tell which facts came from you, so it cannot find or delete them for you; you can report a fact as wrong in the app, or write to us.
  • The sign-in record itself (a random identifier and the times it was created and last used) is deleted when it has not been used for 180 days. A sign-in you keep using is not deleted.
  • The product facts you shared stay in the catalogue as community information; they are not linked to your name or Apple Account. Facts that reached “Community confirmed” stay confirmed after the hashes behind them are gone.

Because the sign-in is random and local to your device, another device gets its own.

Product information is general information

Product and nutrition details are for general information only. They are not an official product label or a guarantee, and may be incomplete, inaccurate or out of date. Always check the package for current information, especially for allergies or intolerances. Stovo does not provide medical or personal dietary advice. Stovo shows no health score of its own; where Open Food Facts supplies a Nutri-Score, NOVA group or nutrient levels, they are shown as that source’s, may be incomplete or incorrect, and say nothing about your health.

Voice entry (optional)

In Shopping and the Pantry you can tap the microphone and say what you need or have. Stovo uses Apple’s speech recognition to turn your words into text, and asks for microphone and speech-recognition permission only when you first use it. If your iPhone can recognize your language on the device, your voice stays on the iPhone. If it cannot, Stovo tells you first and records only if you agree: your voice is then sent to Apple to be turned into text, under Apple’s terms. Stovo does not record, store or send your voice or the text to us. Nothing is added to a list or to your Pantry until you have checked and confirmed it.

Changing things with words (on your device)

In Shopping, the Pantry, the Meal Planner and on a recipe you can type what you bought or used, or ask to move, replace or remove a planned meal, or ask what you can cook. Exact commands (“bought milk”, “what’s low”) are read on your iPhone with fixed word lists in English and German; those are never sent anywhere. For other messages Stovo AI uses two models, whichever suits the task. For finding things inside the app (your lists, Pantry, plan and recipes) it uses Apple’s model on your iPhone where your iPhone supports Apple Intelligence; that runs on the device. For making something new, such as writing a recipe, ideas, changing a recipe or open conversation, it uses ChatGPT from OpenAI (see below), and also where your iPhone has no Apple Intelligence. A photo of a receipt, a written list or food is read on the iPhone.

Before anything changes, Stovo shows what each item is now and what it will be after, asks you to choose when more than one item could match or an amount is missing, and changes nothing until you confirm. “Only me” recipes are never offered or named when you plan for the household.

Afterwards you can undo the whole change for 30 minutes, if none of the things it changed has been edited since (by you or by someone in your household). For this Stovo keeps a short note on your iPhone only: the identifiers, amounts and dates of what it changed, with no names. It is deleted when you undo, when it expires, or when you remove the app, and Stovo does not sync it. The changes themselves are ordinary edits and sync with your household like any other edit.

Stovo AI and ChatGPT (uses OpenAI)

Stovo AI uses ChatGPT (OpenAI) for recipes, ideas, quick changes to a recipe and open conversation. It is available only when you are signed in, and only in app versions that include it; an app version that does not include it uses no AI service. Stovo does not ask before each message. The assistant tells you once, the first time you open it, what ChatGPT is used for. You can turn ChatGPT off at any time in Settings → “Smarter answers with ChatGPT”; then nothing is sent to OpenAI.

What is sent, and when. When you write a message that Stovo AI hands to ChatGPT, the app sends it to Stovo’s server, and Stovo’s server sends it on to OpenAI. Messages about finding things in your own lists, Pantry, plan and recipes are answered by Apple’s model on your iPhone where available and are not sent. Exactly what is sent:

  • the text you typed and the last few messages of the conversation (at most 8 lines);
  • the names of items on your lists and in your Pantry, of low-stock and expiring items, of your recipes (with their cooking time) and of planned meals, and the number of people you usually cook for. Never amounts, notes, ratings, photos, household members’ names, loyalty cards or location;
  • the recipe you are looking at, when you ask to change it;
  • a language code (en or de) and Stovo’s fixed instructions to the model;
  • a one-way (SHA-256) hash of your account identifier, which OpenAI uses as a “safety identifier” to detect abuse. Your account identifier itself, your name and your email address are not sent to OpenAI.

Please do not type personal details: what you type is sent as you wrote it.

Stovo’s server. The request passes through a function on Stovo’s server that checks that you are signed in with a real account. It does not store your message or the answer and does not log them (it logs fixed phrases and status codes only). To enforce limits, Stovo’s database keeps a daily request counter for each person and one overall counter: your account identifier, the day and a number, never the text. Like any server, it handles your IP address in the request.

OpenAI’s handling. Stovo asks OpenAI not to store the response. That does not by itself mean OpenAI keeps nothing. According to OpenAI’s developer documentation, data sent to its API is not used to train or improve its models by default, and abuse-monitoring logs are kept for up to 30 days. So what is sent may be kept by OpenAI for up to 30 days. OpenAI is based in the United States and processes requests on its servers there and elsewhere; Stovo does not choose a data region.

International transfer. OpenAI is outside Switzerland and the European Union. The transfer happens when you use Stovo AI’s ChatGPT features after the notice described above. If you do not want this, turn ChatGPT off in Settings; Apple’s on-device model and the word lists keep working for what they can do, and nothing else in Stovo sends anything to OpenAI.

What comes back. Spoken answers, questions, proposed changes to your lists and recipe suggestions. A recipe is labelled in the recipe itself as “AI suggestion (untested)”. Stovo checks them with fixed rules, not with the model: a request that names something that is not food is refused on your phone before anything is sent, and an answer that does so is dropped; a recipe with a temperature in Fahrenheit is dropped; and a recipe that uses meat, poultry, fish or seafood gets one fixed note written by Stovo, not by the model, from the Swiss Federal Food Safety and Veterinary Office: heat meat, poultry, fish and seafood to at least 70 °C, keep raw meat and fish apart from other food, and use separate boards and utensils for raw and cooked food. These checks are a safety net, not a guarantee: a suggestion can still be wrong or unsafe. Everything it proposes is shown to you first. Recipes start as “Only me” recipes, nothing is saved until you apply it, and you can edit or discard them. Stovo does not check them for allergens and never claims a suggestion is safe for an allergy or a medical diet. Always check cooking times and temperatures against reliable sources.

Basis. Your agreement, which you give by using the ChatGPT features after the notice, and can withdraw at any time in Settings.

Usage analytics (optional)

Stovo can send us usage statistics so we can see how often Stovo is used and which features people use. This is off until you turn it on. Stovo asks once, after you have finished a shopping trip, and you can turn it on or off at any time in Settings → About → Usage Analytics. If you say no, Stovo does not ask again. Before that, nothing is counted, stored or sent. Stovo uses no analytics software package; it sends the statistics itself to PostHog (PostHog Inc., Cloud, EU region), which processes them for us.

What we receive. Once for each day you used Stovo, after that day has ended: the day, the Stovo version, whether it is an App Store or TestFlight version, whether Stovo is installed on your Apple Watch, and counts for that day: how often you opened Stovo, items added and checked off, Pantry items added and stock changes, cards added and opened, taps on widgets, items checked off from widgets and times the Premium screen was shown; and how many items you added in each broad category (for example Dairy or Drinks; categories you created yourself count together as one). We do not receive the time of day you use Stovo. Today’s counts stay on your device until the day is over. We do not collect purchases.

What we never receive. Item names, notes, amounts, list names, category names, card names, numbers or barcodes, your household or account details, your name, email address, contacts or location, your Apple Account, or any device or advertising identifier. There are no records of single items and no screen-by-screen tracking.

Random ID. So that we can tell whether people come back, Stovo creates a random ID when you turn this on. It is not your name, Apple Account, Stovo account or device ID, and we do not link it to any of them. Because it ties the statistics of one installation together over time, we treat the statistics as pseudonymous personal data, not as anonymous data. The ID is kept on your device (it can come back if you restore the device from a backup).

IP address. Like any internet connection, the connection to PostHog carries your device’s IP address. We have set PostHog not to store it, and Stovo asks PostHog not to derive a location from it.

Turning it off. You can turn it off at any time in Settings → About → Usage Analytics. Stovo then stops counting and deletes the random ID and everything that has not been sent yet. Statistics already sent cannot be linked back to you afterwards, so we cannot find or delete them on request. PostHog keeps them for up to one year.

Basis. Your consent, which you can withdraw by turning it off.

Sharing text into Stovo

If you share text from another app (for example a list in Notes) to Stovo, it is handed to Stovo on your device. Stovo shows it for your review before anything is added. It is not sent anywhere.

Item pictures

The small picture in front of an item is chosen on your device from the item’s text, from pictures that are part of the app. Nothing is looked up online and no item text is sent.

Notifications and reminders

Notifications are off until you turn them on in Stovo’s Settings, and Stovo asks iOS for permission only then. You can turn them off in Stovo or in the iOS Settings app at any time.

  • Reminders (shopping and per-list) are local notifications: Stovo schedules them on your device from your lists, and their text — a list name and a number of items — is created on the device.
  • Use Soon (optional) is at most one notice a day, only when something in your Pantry is best before soon. It is created on your device from your Pantry and is a prompt to check the package, not a statement about the food.
  • Your notification choices and per-list reminders are kept on the device only.
  • Stovo does not operate a push notification server and does not collect push tokens.

Stovo Premium

Subscriptions are bought and managed through Apple. Stovo does not receive your Apple Account, payment details or purchase receipts on a server. Stovo reads the subscription’s status from Apple on your device. To give your household Premium, Stovo stores in the household’s synced data an expiry date and an opaque, hashed identifier of the subscription — not your Apple Account. With Apple Family Sharing, Stovo does not see who is in your family.

Siri, Shortcuts and widgets

When you use Stovo with Siri or Shortcuts, your request is handled by Apple and Stovo performs the action on your device, for example adding an item to a list or telling you tonight’s planned dinner. Stovo does not send the request anywhere. Widgets and watch faces show counts, list names and planned meals taken from your device.

What Stovo does not do

  • No advertising.
  • No tracking across apps or websites, and no data brokers.
  • No analytics or usage data sent to the developer unless you turn on Usage Analytics.
  • No third-party code or SDKs that collect data. The services Stovo contacts are Apple (sign-in, purchases, speech recognition when you agree, Siri); Supabase (Stovo’s server and catalogue); Logo.dev, for store logos; Open Food Facts, only if you use the product catalogue; Vercel and Resend, only if you ask for a store to be added; PostHog, only if you turn on Usage Analytics; OpenAI, only for Stovo AI’s ChatGPT features (switchable in Settings); and the websites you import recipes from, only when you import a link.
  • No selling or sharing of your data.

Settings kept on your device

Your appearance and color theme, unit and currency settings, your Apple Watch card choice, your notification choices and reminders, whether Siri & Shortcuts is switched on, which list is selected and which household is active are kept on the device only. They are not synced and not sent anywhere.

Diagnostics

If Stovo ever cannot open its data, it shows a recovery screen with technical details — the error, the size of its data file and how much storage is free on the device. These details stay on your device. You can choose to copy them and send them to support yourself; nothing is sent automatically.

Apple’s “required reason” APIs

Apple asks apps to declare why they use certain system features. Stovo declares:

  • User defaults (reasons CA92.1 and 1C8F.1) — to store the app’s own settings, and to hand a tap on a widget from the widget to the app through their shared App Group, on the device.
  • File timestamps and metadata (reason C617.1) — to read information about Stovo’s own data file inside its app container, such as its size for the recovery details.
  • Disk space (reason 7D9E.1) — to include the free storage in the recovery details, which you may choose to send in a support request.

None of this information leaves your device unless you send it yourself.

Keeping and deleting your data

  • Your data is kept until you delete it. You can delete items, lists, recipes and pantry entries in the app at any time. Deleting Stovo removes its data from the device, but not your account on Stovo’s server.
  • Delete your account in Settings → Account → Delete Account. Households you own that have other active members are handed to the member who joined first and keep their shared data. Households you own with nobody else in them are deleted with everything in them. Your account is deleted, and with it your memberships, your cards, recipe folders, ratings, private notes, “Only me” recipes, settings and invitations. Your name is removed from items you added in households that remain. If you signed in with Apple, Stovo asks Apple to revoke the sign-in. Supabase may keep routine backups and logs for a limited time after deletion.
  • Deletions are kept on the server as markers for up to 180 days, so that your other devices learn of them, and are then purged.
  • Leaving a shared household removes its data from your devices; the owner keeps it.
  • Product facts you added stay in the catalogue as described in The random sign-in used for sharing; for a question about something you shared, write to info@stovo.ch.
  • Usage statistics already sent to PostHog cannot be linked to you after you turn Usage Analytics off, and cannot be deleted on request; PostHog keeps them for up to one year.
  • Settings offers a readable text export of your open shopping items, Pantry and recipes. It is a copy to read, not a backup you can restore.
  • Stovo AI: Stovo does not keep your messages or the answers. OpenAI’s own retention is described above.

Your rights

Under the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection and, where it applies to you, the EU General Data Protection Regulation, you can ask for access to your data, for correction or deletion, for a copy of the data you provided, and to restrict or object to its use, and you can withdraw a consent you gave at any time (for example for voice entry, usage statistics or ChatGPT in Stovo AI). Write to info@stovo.ch; we answer within 30 days. You can complain to the Swiss Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (FDPIC) or, in the EU, to your local supervisory authority.

Legal bases. Contract (running the app and your account), consent (voice entry off the device, usage statistics, ChatGPT uses) and legitimate interest (security and abuse limits for the catalogue).

Where data is processed

Stovo’s own server is in Switzerland (Zurich). Other providers may process data outside Switzerland and the European Union: Apple; PostHog (EU region); Open Food Facts; Logo.dev; Vercel and Resend (store requests only); GitHub (this website); and OpenAI, in the United States, for Stovo AI’s ChatGPT features only. Each receives only what this policy says it receives. For OpenAI, see Stovo AI and ChatGPT: the transfer takes place only with your explicit consent for each request.

Children
